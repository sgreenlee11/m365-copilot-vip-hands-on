A day in the life with Copilot

Each prompt below tells you exactly how to make it recur — schedule it inside Copilot, block it on your calendar, or run it off a trigger. Copy it, replace the highlighted brackets, act on it.

Do this first

Run once, not on a schedule. Copilot remembers it, and every prompt below gets sharper because you stop re-explaining who you are.

00

Teach Copilot who you are

4 minCopilot MemoryOne time only

Why: Most disappointing Copilot answers are missing context, not intelligence. Set this once and your tone, priorities, and stakeholders are already known.

Prompt 00 · Personal context
Remember this about me for all future conversations. I am [title] at [company], accountable for [the functions you own]. My direct reports are [names]. My most important stakeholders are [board, CEO, top customers, regulators] and what each cares about most is [their priorities]. My recurring rhythm is [weekly business review, monthly operating review, quarterly earnings]. When you draft for me, default to direct and decision-first: lead with the recommendation, then the evidence, then the risks. No marketing language. Plain English that both a store manager and an analyst would understand.
No scheduling needed

Run it once in Copilot Chat. Revisit it when your priorities or stakeholders change — roughly once a quarter.

The rhythm

Times are a suggested default. Shift them to fit your day — keep the sequence.

7:45 AM Daily
01

The morning signal scan

5 minCopilot ChatSchedule in Copilot

Why: Before the first meeting, know the three things that actually moved overnight — not the 40 things in your inbox.

Prompt 01 · Signal scan
Review my email, chats, and meetings from the last 24 hours across [the functions or stakeholder groups you own]. Identify the three developments most likely to need my attention today. For each one, tell me what changed, why it matters now, the decision or nudge you recommend from me, and the source that supports it. Then show my calendar for today and flag where those three items collide with a meeting. Separate confirmed facts from your interpretation.
Schedule it in Copilot

Send the prompt in Copilot Chat, hover over it, and choose Schedule this prompt → every weekday, 7:45 AM. Turn on the email notification. The answer is waiting in your Chats list before you open the laptop.

30 MIN BEFORE Trigger
02

Prep me for the room

3 minCopilot ChatDon't schedule

Why: The difference between attending a meeting and steering one is two better questions. Run it before any meeting where a decision is expected.

Prompt 02 · Prep me for the room
I have a meeting with [person or team] at [time]. Pull the last 60 days of email and Teams messages I have with them. Tell me their top concern right now, what I committed to last time, what is still outstanding between us, and the three questions they are most likely to ask me. Do not infer anyone's position unless the content supports it. Finish with two assumptions the group should test.
Trigger it, don't schedule it

The meeting is the trigger, so a fixed time makes no sense — and scheduled prompts can't reference a specific meeting anyway. Save it to your Prompt Gallery (hover the prompt → bookmark icon) so it is one click before you walk in.

12:30 PM Daily
03

Midday inbox triage

5 minCopilot ChatSchedule in Copilot

Why: You are the bottleneck on more threads than you think. This separates what only you can decide from what your team should already own.

Prompt 03 · Decide, note, delegate
Look at my inbox and chats from the last [2] days. Group what is waiting on me into three lists: decisions needed from me, including who is asking and by when; FYI only, important context but no action required; and delegatable, what my team can handle with a one-line forward from me. Rank the first list by who is blocked. For the top three, draft a short reply in my voice that answers the question, makes the decision, or sets a date by which I will. Keep each under 120 words and do not send anything.
Schedule it in Copilot

Hover the prompt → Schedule this prompt → every weekday, 12:30 PM. You come back from lunch to a sorted queue and three drafts to edit.

4:45 PM Daily
04

The close-out ledger

5 minCopilot ChatSchedule in Copilot

Why: Decisions decay overnight. Capture what was decided, who owns it, and what you personally committed to before you close the laptop.

Prompt 04 · Decisions and owners
Summarize my meetings and chats from today into four lists. Decisions made — specific commitments, not themes. Action items — named owner, specific next step, and a due date where one was stated. Open questions we are carrying forward. And the commitments I made myself, flagging which ones have no date attached. Cite the meeting or message behind each item, and note anything that was discussed but explicitly not decided.
Schedule it in Copilot

Hover the prompt → Schedule this prompt → every weekday, 4:45 PM. Microsoft lists this as a flagship scheduled-prompt use case. Paste the commitments list straight into your task list.

MON 9:00 AM Weekly
05

Monday numbers check

15 minAnalystCalendar block

Why: Start the week arguing with the data, not collecting it. Attach the one recurring report you already receive and let Analyst do the first pass.

Prompt 05 · Trend and outlier pass
Using the attached [weekly performance file], analyze performance across [the dimension you manage — region, store, product, team]. Identify the top and bottom five performers, the three clearest trends, and any outliers or data-quality issues I should not trust. Show the calculation behind each finding and build one chart that best explains the story. Finish with the two questions I should take to my leadership team this week.
Calendar block — this one can't be scheduled

Scheduled prompts can't attach a specific file, so this one needs you in the seat. Recurring Outlook hold, Mondays 9:00 AM, 30 minutes before your leadership meeting. Save the report to the same OneDrive folder every week so it is one click to attach.

FRI 2:00 PM Weekly
06

Friday digest & stakeholder update

15 minCopilot ChatSchedule in Copilot

Why: One prompt gives you the week in review and the update your board, chair, or team is already expecting. Run it, edit it, send it.

Prompt 06 · Week in review
Summarize my past week across email, chats, meetings, and documents, grouped by [stakeholder or workstream — customers, people, finance, operations]. For each group give me what progressed, what stalled, and what needs a decision next week. Then draft a concise update for [your audience] in my voice: five bullets of progress, two risks with the mitigation underway, and one clear ask. Keep it under 300 words, cite your sources below the draft, and end by telling me what I missed this week that I should have been paying attention to.
Schedule it in Copilot

Hover the prompt → Schedule this prompt → weekly, Friday 2:00 PM. Copy the draft into Outlook or Word to finish it — scheduled prompts return text, not files.

How to schedule a prompt

Four of the six run themselves once you set them up. Do not adopt all six at once — schedule 01 this week, then add the rest.

Schedule it in Copilot
  1. Send the prompt in Copilot Chat, Teams, or Outlook.
  2. Hover over the prompt you just sent → Schedule this prompt.
  3. Choose daily, weekdays, or weekly, set the time, and turn on the email notification.
  4. Answers arrive in your Chats list in bold. Manage them under … → Scheduled prompts.
Know the limits
  1. Requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot license.
  2. Cap of 10 scheduled prompts, and each expires after roughly 15 runs — re-create the ones that earn their keep.
  3. Text answers only: no Word, Excel, or PowerPoint files, and no attaching a specific file or meeting.
  4. That limit is exactly why 02 and 05 stay manual.
For the two that stay manual
  1. Save both to your Prompt Gallery: hover the prompt → bookmark icon.
  2. They reappear under Your Prompts — one click, no retyping.
  3. Give 05 a recurring Monday hold; 02 runs off the meeting itself.
  4. Share your saved prompts with your leadership team to standardize the habit.
